# §01 · OPENING CINEMATIC ◆

⟨ §01 · v1.0 · touched 2026-07-31 · confidence: ASSUMED ⟩

## Mission

**Reward gamers for their time.**

Billions of hours go into mobile and casual games. Nearly all the economic value from those hours is captured by ad networks, publishers, and app stores. The player receives entertainment and almost never receives meaningful economic value. Arena exists to route a real share of that value back to the people generating it.

## The premise

Players check in at a physical venue. They compete in short, unpredictable skill-based micro-games they could not have practiced for. Performance produces rank. Rank is public, persistent, and the primary reward. Prizes — real, physical, sourced at negotiated cost — arrive as proof that the mission is real.

## What the product actually is

A **reward engine wearing an esports platform**. The games are the distribution mechanism, not the product. The product is standing, anticipation, and a local scene worth showing up to.

## The three-sided exchange

| Party | Gives | Receives |
|---|---|---|
| **Players** | Time, attention, demonstrated skill | Status, and prizes won on merit |
| **Sponsors & venues** | Prizes, placement fees, in-kind inventory | Verified foot traffic, verified attention, preference signal |
| **Arena** | Infrastructure, procurement leverage, the scene itself | Margin on the spread between the two |

## Positioning

Robin Hood, stated plainly. The platform does not pretend advertising is enjoyable — it says out loud that advertising is how prizes get bought. Revenue is not hidden because revenue is the mechanism of the mission. Platform and players sit on the same side; the extractive advertising economy sits on the other.

## Why now

The build was economically impossible for a solo operator until generative models collapsed the cost of producing original games, original narrative, and the compliance scaffolding around both. Concept work dates to 2018. The remaining obstacles are legal structure and go-to-market — not engineering.

## Naming ruling

**Arena** = the company, the build layer, the operating entity.
**The Arcade Realm** = the world, the narrative layer, the player-facing brand.

Both names persist by design; they name different things. This resolves the pending DECISION in `STATE_LEDGER.md` and should be logged at next close.

# §03 · THE FIVE WARDS ◆

⟨ §03 · v1.0 · touched 2026-07-31 · confidence: IMPORTED ⟩

Five value ecosystems, walled from each other. One brand, five separate value-mechanics, each with distinct regulatory weight. **Activation is strictly left to right.** A ward does not open because it would be lucrative; it opens because the wards to its left are proven and its own gate has cleared.

```
   A ──────▶ B ──────▶ C ──────▶ D ──────▶ E
 STATUS   SPONSOR    SKILL     DIGITAL    TOKEN
          PRIZES    CONTEST  COLLECTIBLES  ────
  none    contract  sweeps +   IP + cons.  securities +
  legal      law    skill-gaming protection  money trans.
  surface           law
  ▲ LIVE   ▲ NEAR    ⊘ MID     ⊘ CAUTION  ⊘ LAST /
                                            MAYBE NEVER
```

## Ward A — Status

**Mechanic:** Free play, public rank, persistent standing.
**Legal surface:** None. No consideration, no prize, no chance.
**State:** Launch now. Everything needed to prove the core loop lives entirely inside this ward.

The strategic point: the thing that actually drives retention — rank — carries no regulatory weight at all. Arena can prove its central hypothesis before touching a single regulated surface.

## Ward B — Sponsor Prizes

**Mechanic:** Prizes funded by sponsors and venues, awarded on skill, no purchase or entry fee.
**Legal surface:** Contract law. Sponsor agreements, official rules, fulfillment obligations.
**State:** Near-term. Opens once Ward A demonstrates a working loop at one venue.

## Ward C — Skill Contest

**Mechanic:** Paid entry into higher-value contests.
**Legal surface:** Sweepstakes and skill-gaming law. Per-state variation is severe.
**State:** ⊘ Mid-term. Requires the C3 skill-gradient evidence record and per-jurisdiction switching (C2) shipped and tested.

## Ward D — Digital Collectibles

**Mechanic:** Persistent digital objects tied to standing and lore.
**Legal surface:** IP and consumer protection.
**State:** ⊘ Cautious. Not scheduled.

## Ward E — Token / Crypto

**Mechanic:** Transferable digital value.
**Legal surface:** Securities law and money transmission.
**State:** ⊘ Last, possibly never. Behind the Arena Coin counsel gate. See §12.

## The wall rule

Value never crosses a ward boundary. Standing earned in A does not convert to value in B, C, D, or E — it makes a player **eligible** for things. A prize awarded in B is not a payout on anything earned in A. Each ward is legally self-contained and can be switched off independently without collapsing the ones to its left.

# §04 · FIREWALL DOCTRINE ◆

⟨ §04 · v1.0 · touched 2026-07-31 · confidence: IMPORTED ⟩

Non-negotiable. Binds every agent, every surface, every document — including this one.

## Rule 1 — Eligibility ≠ Entitlement

Standing and rank-tokens make a player **eligible** for things. They never **entitle** a player to a payout, and they never convert to currency or crypto.

This is the load-bearing rule. It is what keeps standing out of securities analysis, and it is what makes the paper vault possible: a displayed prize catalog carries near-zero liability because eligibility creates no obligation until a win event fires.

## Rule 2 — Marketing is a regulated surface

No copy anywhere — lore, UI, landing pages, Discord, campaign material, decks — may frame anything as an investment, promise appreciation, or imply that early participants profit.

**Forbidden frames:** investment · appreciates in value · returns · early holders profit · guaranteed prize · buy in now · get in early — and any paraphrase carrying the same meaning.

**Safe frames:** mission · aspiration · standing · status · proof of mission · eligibility · recognition.

The rule is enforced mechanically. C1 specifies a forbidden-frames linter (regex plus classifier) that L4's Loremaster calls as a hard output gate and that runs in CI over static copy.

**Why it exists, in plain language:** if a person hands over money or value expecting profit from the efforts of others, that is a securities question regardless of what the thing is called. Copy is evidence. A future contributor who softens this rule for marketing energy is creating liability, not upside. Do not soften it.

## Rule 3 — Per-jurisdiction switches

Anything touching paid entry or value ships able to be disabled per-state, from day one, before any such feature is enabled anywhere. C2 implements the switch; F3 carries the stub.

## Rule 4 — Fiction never touches money

Applies to this document, the lore layer, and every extract. RPG and mythic framing may name, organize, and give tone. Any sentence describing money, eligibility, obligation, or return is written literally.

"The Vault" as a section title is fine. "Sealed Caches open when the Realm awakens" is fine in lore. Neither may ever appear in a sentence that a reader could mistake for a description of what they receive or when.

## Standing disclaimer

This document and all compliance work describe what to build and what to ask a licensed attorney. Nothing here is a legal green light. Both open gates in `STATE_LEDGER.md` are cleared by counsel, not by this plan.

# §16 · SAVE & AMEND ◆

⟨ §16 · v1.0 · touched 2026-07-31 · confidence: — ⟩

## Truth and mirrors

This markdown file is truth. The HTML codex and the PDF are **generated renderers** — read-only, one-way flow, never hand-edited. Same discipline as `STATE_LEDGER.md` → `CLAUDE.md`. If a renderer disagrees with this file, this file wins and the renderer is stale.

## Versioning

**Major** (v1.0 → v2.0) — a canon spine section changed. Requires a founder ruling logged to `STATE_LEDGER.md` DECISIONS.
**Minor** (v1.0 → v1.1) — a body section was added, revised, or retired.
**Section versions** are independent and live in the section stamp.

## The amendment grammar

```
AMENDMENT: §NN
TYPE: [ADD | REVISE | RETIRE | RERATE]
CURRENT: <what it says now>
PROPOSED: <what it should say>
BASIS: <observation | calculation | source>
CANON CHECK: <spine items touched — or NONE>
CONFIDENCE: [OBSERVED | MODELED | ASSUMED | IMPORTED]
```

Same shape as a STATUS BLOCK, deliberately. One status grammar everywhere.

**RERATE** exists so confidence can move without content changing. The day the first venue runs, a dozen STAT BLOCK rows go ASSUMED → OBSERVED and nothing else about the plan changes. That is the single most valuable amendment type this document supports.

## The canon check

Before any amendment merges, it is checked against the canon spine — §01, §03, §04, §16. **An amendment that contradicts the spine is rejected, not merged.** It can only proceed by first amending the spine through a founder ruling.

This is the L2 contradiction-check applied to the plan instead of the lore, and it is the same operating principle running one level up: generate freely at the edges, gate hard in the middle.

## Who may propose

Anyone. The founder, an agent, a contributor, a venue operator with an observation. Proposal is open; merge is gated.

The codex renderer may **emit** amendment blocks; it may never **write** them. Contributions come back as paste-able blocks through the one manual heartbeat, exactly as the ledger does. Two write surfaces means two truths, and that failure mode is already documented.
