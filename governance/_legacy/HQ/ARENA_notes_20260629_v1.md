## 1. The Premise, as Myth

The real mission is "reward gamers for their time." Here is that idea wearing a myth:

There is a force in the world that feeds on attention and gives nothing back. It is not named as a villain with a face — it is a *condition*, ambient, the way the tide is not a person. Call it **the Drain** (placeholder). The Drain takes hours and returns numbness; it counts your time as its own.

The Realm is what forms where people refuse that. It is not a place on a map exactly — it surfaces at thresholds, in rooms where people gather to *play for something that is theirs*. When players compete and their skill is witnessed and recorded, a small amount of time is reclaimed from the Drain. Rank is the visible proof of reclaimed time. The leaderboard is a ledger of hours that were spent *on purpose*.

The "us vs. them" you've always wanted is here, but it's mythologized, not preachy: **the Realm vs. the Drain.** Players aren't customers; they're the ones reclaiming what's theirs. The platform isn't a company in-world; it's the *infrastructure of reclamation* — the thing that keeps the ledger honest.

## 2. The Fixed Truths (the canon spine — these can NEVER be contradicted)

These 8 anchor the contradiction-check in L2. Generated lore that violates any of these is rejected. Edit this list carefully — it's the load-bearing part.

1. **The Drain is a condition, never a character.** It has no face, no dialogue, no single avatar. It can be described, resisted, and measured — never met or defeated in a single act. (This keeps the myth open-ended and un-resolvable, which is what makes it last.)
2. **Rank is reclaimed time made visible.** Standing in the Realm always derives from witnessed skill, never from purchase, luck, or favor.
3. **The Realm has no center and no owner.** It surfaces wherever players gather; no faction, person, or place "rules" it.
4. **Witnessing is sacred.** A score that wasn't witnessed (validated) isn't real. The ledger only records what can be proven. (This is your anti-cheat principle, mythologized.)
5. **The Caches are real and they are sealed.** Promised rewards exist as sealed Caches that open only when the Realm reaches true milestones. *(See §5 — and read the firewall note there carefully. The Caches are a mission promise, never a financial one.)*
6. **No one is owed a Cache.** Reaching a milestone opens a Cache for the Realm; standing makes you *eligible* to share in what opens. Eligibility is never entitlement. (This is the Firewall Doctrine written into the myth itself.)
7. **The first players are remembered.** Those who reclaimed time before it was easy hold a permanent place in the records — a standing that later arrivals cannot retroactively buy. (This is your founding-cohort value, and it's *status*, not equity.)
8. **The story is found, never told.** The Realm's history arrives in fragments — glyphs, logs, half-myths. No single source explains it. Questions outrun answers, always. (This licenses the mystery-box discipline in L3.)

## 3. Tone & Voice

- **Mythic, fragmentary, a little archaic — but never solemn to the point of silliness.** Think "found inscriptions and field-logs," not high-fantasy narration.
- Lore reads like it was *discovered*, not authored: partial, weathered, occasionally contradictory at the surface (but never violating the fixed truths beneath).
- **Reveal questions faster than answers.** A good fragment makes a player want to screenshot it and argue about it, not feel like they've been handed exposition.
- **Off-limits:** anything that frames the Realm as a product, anything that breaks the fourth wall into "app" language inside lore, and — critically — **anything that frames Caches, rank, or standing as investments, returns, or appreciating value** (see §5).
- Humor exists at the edges — in faction rivalries, in the voice of specific fragments — but the core myth stays earnest.

## 4. The Factions (seeds — players will shape these over time)

Three to start. Each is a *stance toward reclaimed time*, which gives them built-in rivalry without a scripted war. Rename freely.

- **The Tally** (placeholder) — believe the ledger is everything; rank is the only truth; play to be recorded. Cold, precise, competitive. Their flaw: they can forget *why* the time was worth reclaiming.
- **The Embergames** (placeholder) — believe play is the point and the ledger is just a side-effect; they chase joy and spectacle. Warm, chaotic, beloved. Their flaw: they undervalue the standing they earn.
- **The Quiet Hours** (placeholder) — believe the Realm should stay hidden and small, that scale invites the Drain back in. Secretive, principled, suspicious of milestones. Their flaw: their caution can curdle into gatekeeping.

The tension between "the ledger matters most / the joy matters most / the hiddenness matters most" is a three-way that never resolves — perfect for long-running, player-driven arcs. Players align; the Loremaster (L4) writes real player behavior into faction records.

## 5. The Caches & The Prophecy Frame  ⚠️ FIREWALL-CRITICAL

This is the part that touches your hardest legal constraint. Read it as both lore *and* compliance.

**In-world:** Deferred rewards exist as **sealed Caches**. A Cache opens when the Realm crosses a true milestone — a number of souls reclaimed, total hours witnessed, cities awakened. When a Cache opens, those with standing share in what it holds. The opening of the First Cache is a *prophesied event*: the counter is visible, it ticks with real activity, and when it reaches the threshold, the Realm celebrates a milestone reached together.

**Why this is structured exactly this way (the firewall):**
- A Cache is **proof of mission at scale**, framed as a *collective milestone celebration* — never as a payout you're owed, never as a return on anything, never as a thing that "appreciates."
- Standing makes you **eligible**; it never **entitles** you and never converts to money or crypto. (Fixed Truths #6.)
- The prophecy counter measures *mission progress* (the Realm growing), not the value of a holding.
- **Forbidden framings, in lore and everywhere else:** "investment," "returns," "early holders profit," "appreciates," "buy in," "guaranteed prize." The Loremaster agent (L4) is gated against these by the C1 linter. You must hold the same line in your own copy, Discord, and landing pages.

The actual reward, when a Cache opens, is delivered through the real-world legal structure (sponsor-funded prizes under contract law — Ecosystem B), governed by the eligibility-≠-entitlement rule. **The myth and the mission align: the prophecy fulfilling *is* the first prize event — proof you did the thing you set out to do.** That's the whole strategy in one image, and it stays clean as long as the framing above never slips.
